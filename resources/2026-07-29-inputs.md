# Video-platform research inputs

**Date reviewed:** 2026-07-29  
**Status:** Kickoff research. Each item has a use, reject, or test decision. Vendor claims are not treated as verified integration behavior until tested in the exact workflow.

## Machina: AI video studio in Claude Code

Source supplied by Erin: sponsored X article by Machina / `@EXM7777` describing Higgsfield, Claude Code, Seedance 2.0, style contracts, frame-first production, prompt templates, parallel generation, and FFmpeg assembly.

Decision: **Use selected production principles. Test Higgsfield. Reject unattended volume as the default.**

Carry forward:

- extract a style contract from real film references;
- approve frames before motion;
- lock character, wardrobe, location, and recurring-object references;
- make motion prompts describe a physical event and camera behavior;
- log prompts, models, results, and keep/kill decisions;
- use low resolution for exploration and upscale only after picture lock;
- let Erin own taste while the agent operates approved production loops.

Do not adopt blindly:

- sponsored claims and exact CLI examples require live verification;
- fifty retries per shot and overnight fleets conflict with Erin's review-first workflow;
- parallel generation begins only after the shot family, references, acceptance criteria, concurrency, and total spend are approved;
- a text-file FFmpeg montage does not replace a visible timeline, contextual review, or crafted transitions;
- one-action prompts are a useful default, not a universal restriction on longer narrative sequences.

Verified Higgsfield boundaries:

- [Higgsfield CLI](https://higgsfield.ai/cli) officially supports account authentication, agent access to multiple models, generation history, reference reuse, and MCP/CLI operation without a user-managed API key.
- [Higgsfield Supercomputer guide](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/higgsfield-supercomputer-guide) says Supercomputer is paid and is not a frame-level editor.
- [Higgsfield terms](https://higgsfield.ai/terms-of-use-agreement) allow credits to be applied to agent tasks, memory, storage, and other designated features.
- [Seedance Unlimited documentation](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/seedance-unlimited) says that add-on is web-only and unavailable through CLI, MCP, Canvas, or Supercomputer.

Required proof before adoption:

1. Authenticate without exposing a key to the agent.
2. List the real models and schemas available to Erin's account.
3. Upload a local still and generate one capped test clip.
4. Download it to a deterministic project path.
5. Confirm cost preview, cancellation, run history, and commercial-use record.
6. Compare output and total cost with the existing Fal path.

## Cheap video witness through Gemini and OpenRouter

Source supplied by Erin: X article by Andres / `@WiFiMoneyGuy` proposing Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite as inexpensive visual analysis for a frontier reasoning agent.

Decision: **Test as first-pass observation, never final creative judgment.**

Verified:

- [Google's video-understanding guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding) says Gemini samples video at 1 FPS by default, includes audio, adds timestamps, and uses about 300 tokens per second at default media resolution or 100 at low resolution.
- The same guide warns that 1 FPS can miss rapid motion and quick cuts.
- [OpenRouter video-input documentation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/overview/multimodal/videos) supports compatible models through `video_url`, including URL or base64 data for local MP4, MPEG, MOV, and WebM files.
- [OpenRouter's Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite page](https://openrouter.ai/google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite) currently lists $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.40 per million output tokens.

Proposed role:

- produce timestamped observations of cuts, visible actions, on-screen text, framing, audio events, and obvious continuity failures;
- populate the contextual review hub with a cheap first-pass report;
- send decision-critical frames, fast transitions, facial continuity, geometry, and premium-quality judgments to the primary reasoning model or human review;
- compare witness reports against Erin's judgment on real clips before trusting automated acceptance criteria.

Security note: OpenRouter uses an API key. The eventual runner must read it through a local secret store or runtime environment without displaying it to the agent.

## Stanley Studio

Source: [studio.getstanley.ai](https://studio.getstanley.ai/)

Decision: **Use as an interaction reference; test before considering it as an editing dependency.**

Relevant ideas:

- chat commands alter an editable timeline rather than returning an opaque finished video;
- one workspace exposes media, sounds, captions, text, transitions, and settings;
- music appears on the timeline and can be ducked under voice;
- alternate takes remain available;
- transcript edits can change the cut;
- one shared review link keeps collaborators on the same version.

Limits for Erin's platform:

- Stanley is positioned around editing footage that already exists, especially creator and social footage;
- it does not establish the preproduction, model-routing, reference, prompt, generation, and continuity system Erin needs;
- its claims about automatic quality and music choice need testing with an actual project.

## Palmier Pro

Source: [github.com/palmier-io/palmier-pro](https://github.com/palmier-io/palmier-pro)

Decision: **Highest-priority build-versus-adopt test for the timeline/editor layer.**

Verified from the repository:

- native Swift macOS editor with a Premiere-inspired timeline;
- local MCP server at `http://127.0.0.1:19789/mcp` for Codex, Claude, and Cursor;
- built-in generation references Seedance, Kling, and Nano Banana Pro;
- editor and MCP server are open source under GPLv3, while generative AI processing is closed source;
- editor and MCP use are free, while generation requires login and subscription;
- requires macOS 26 Tahoe on Apple Silicon.

Required test:

1. Confirm Erin's Mac and OS are compatible.
2. Inspect the MCP tool schemas rather than relying on the README.
3. Import the Arkhai v006 project assets or a small duplicate test project.
4. Verify frame-accurate trim, transitions, audio lanes, captions, export, and reversible agent edits.
5. Review GPLv3 implications before copying code into a differently licensed product.

## ScriptBreak

Source: [github.com/wassermanproductions/scriptbreak](https://github.com/wassermanproductions/scriptbreak)

Decision: **Strong reference and possible reusable base for entertainment preproduction.**

Verified from the repository:

- imports Fountain, Final Draft XML, PDF, and text screenplays;
- creates scene cards, shot lists, character/location/prop bibles, a whole-film timeline, and project look fields;
- exports readable Markdown prompt packs;
- saves projects as readable, diffable JSON;
- provides a headless MCP server for Codex and other clients;
- runs locally without an account or API key;
- uses the Apache 2.0 license.

Best fit:

- entertainment mode and longer narrative work;
- source model for scenes, continuity entities, prompt context, and timeline overview;
- possible integration or adaptation after a live workflow test.

It does not replace the generation queue, contextual clip review, audio timeline, or final editor.

## CutScript

Source: [github.com/DataAnts-AI/CutScript](https://github.com/DataAnts-AI/CutScript)

Decision: **Strongest reusable base for replacing Erin's Descript subscription; inspect and test before choosing it as the editor foundation.**

Verified from the repository:

- local-first Electron and React desktop app with a FastAPI backend;
- WhisperX word-level transcription and alignment;
- transcript-based video edits, undo/redo, waveform timeline, and keyboard controls;
- FFmpeg stream-copy and re-encoded exports up to 4K;
- filler-word removal, short-clip creation, speaker diarization, and word-level captions;
- DeepFilterNet audio cleanup;
- encrypted API-key storage and local `.cutscript` project save/load;
- MIT license.

Why it matters:

- It covers much of the Descript-shaped editing engine without requiring a subscription.
- Its web technology is easier to adapt than a Swift-only editor if Erin later wants a cross-platform product.
- It does not yet provide the AI-generation production model, scene-context review, prompt/version history, character bibles, transition planning, or model routing required by this project.

Required test:

1. Import a real talking-head, podcast, or screen-recording project Erin previously edited in Descript.
2. Measure transcription quality, sync, transcript-edit correctness, filler removal, audio cleanup, caption quality, and export speed.
3. Inspect project portability, failure recovery, memory use on Erin's Mac, and whether the local backend stays reliable.

## Descript

Source: [Descript pricing and feature matrix](https://www.descript.com/pricing)

Decision: **Use as a replacement benchmark, not as the architecture. Interview Erin about the subset she actually uses.**

Current benchmark capabilities include transcript-based editing, multitrack audio, screen recording, remote recording, transcription, captions, Studio Sound, filler and retake removal, clips, voice generation, video generation, and an AI co-editor. Current individual paid tiers begin at $16 per month annually or $24 monthly, with 1080p export on Hobbyist and 4K on Creator.

Do not rebuild every listed feature. Replacement succeeds when Erin can cancel because her own repeated workflows are covered with equal or lower friction.

Erin's required replacement subset from the kickoff interview:

- combine audio, interviews, screen recordings, images, video, and text;
- transcript-based editing and filler-word removal;
- agent-assisted identification and creation of strong short clips;
- teleprompter from an approved script;
- captions and long-form/short-form export;
- preserve full interview recordings while editing derivatives.

Remote interview recording is not an initial requirement because Google Meet can provide the source recording.

## CapCut

Source: [CapCut Desktop](https://www.capcut.com/tools/desktop-video-editor)

Decision: **Borrow interaction patterns and speed; reject an effects-first product center.**

Carry forward:

- direct timeline manipulation with immediate playback;
- visible video, voice, music, SFX, captions, and transition lanes;
- keyframes and curves for precise motion and transition polish;
- searchable local media by object, dialogue, and person;
- automatic captions and transcript-based editing;
- quick aspect-ratio versions that keep the subject framed;
- keyboard shortcuts and fast export;
- optional templates for repetitive marketing formats.

Do not copy:

- a crowded effects marketplace;
- trend-driven decoration as the default;
- automatic style decisions that weaken the project's style contract;
- platform publishing or growth automation inside the core editor.

## Viral-format research and distribution loop

Source supplied by Erin: Fokki / `@0x_fokki`, "How I Predict Viral Videos Before They Explode."

Decision: **Save as an optional marketing-research lane. Do not treat revenue projections or virality thresholds as verified.**

Useful mechanics:

- compare a video's views with its own channel median rather than raw views alone;
- separate repeatable format from disposable topic;
- capture the exact first frame and opening text;
- tag each produced video with its format and hook;
- feed actual performance back into future creative decisions.

Boundaries:

- outlier score thresholds, two-week trend windows, income claims, channel sale values, and cost projections are unsupported in the supplied article and need independent data;
- copying a proven format can help short-form marketing but should not govern brand films or entertainment storytelling;
- automated posting remains a separate, approval-gated distribution system.
