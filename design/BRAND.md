# Videyo interface brief

## Feeling, Erin's words

> "beautiful, inspiring, futuristic interface for video generation"

> "i like dark mode generally but I want it to feel light and open"

> "should feel friendly - like lean into the yo video part - like yo friend do this"

> "more imaginative world building/game tool but for video creation but has all the tools of a professional film software"

> "they need to think this is cool more than this is beautiful"

> "your projects are creative it should feel like you're building with a friend. it should be fun by your side at every step and make it better and compliment your skillsets"

## Design direction

- Dark foundation with open composition and luminous media.
- Creative process first, production evidence second, generated media inside both.
- Videyo is named and visible.
- Professional film tools are present without turning the first screen into a dense control panel.
- "Yo" behaves like a creative companion with useful context, not a support chatbot.
- The target reaction is "this is cool, I want to make something with it."

## Universal rejects

- Dark blue or purple gradients.
- Dense black control panels.
- Generic prompt box as the entire product.
- Card grids and pill labels on every object.
- Cute mascot treatment that weakens professional credibility.
- Decorative AI glow with no relation to project state.

## Signature artifact

The "Yo signal" is a responsive companion mark that points to the next useful creative action. It can move through the project, timeline, and provenance record without becoming a chat bubble.

Yo can see the item the user points toward and has access to a portable knowledge graph of scenes, assets, notes, prompts, decisions, themes, and unused ideas. The graph is Yo's memory, not a separate technical dashboard.

## Concept round

- A, Companion Orbit: the project is a world in the center; Yo orbits the work and the timeline anchors it.
- B, Playable Timeline: the film timeline becomes a navigable world map; Yo acts as a co-director inside it.
- C, Director's Table: media, evidence, and tools sit on one open spatial desk; Yo leaves concise creative nudges beside the work.

## Erin's reaction, 2026-08-03

> "a & c seem very similar"

> "I like the journey timeline + a knowledge graph + you can click to point to a section to regenerate that"

> "i want it to look like a new platform not like typical video editing platforms"

> "it should feel creative and game like"

Decision: develop B, Playable Timeline. A and C are the same underlying composition and do not count as distinct directions. The production interface uses a journey graph as its main navigation. Selecting a story beat reveals its connected knowledge and allows targeted regeneration with preserved lineage. Traditional editing controls remain secondary.

Follow-on direction: treat story beats as creative quests. Each quest has a challenge, connected knowledge, output, and completion state. Completing a quest opens the next creative possibility. Completed quests remain revisitable and can be regenerated without changing the rest of the project. The treatment stays professionally credible rather than becoming a cartoon game skin.

## Failed real-interface pass and correction

Erin's reaction:

> "where is the knowledge graph?"

> "this looks kinda boring"

> "at the beginning it should be like making a character sheet and evolving the plot"

> "a cool animation would be a knowledge graph that unwinds into a clear story line/timeline"

> "chat should be a little floater/comment box that can follow with you wherever you might click"

Correction: the visible graph is now the main creative mechanism. Characters, locations, ideas, and scenes move from relational space into story order. The first quest creates the cast and establishes how character choices affect the plot. Yo stays visible as a floating contextual thread and responds to comments on the selected object.

Relationship lines remain visible and attached to their nodes throughout the graph-to-story animation. Story order adds a narrative thread without replacing the underlying connections.

Yo is visually tethered to the selected graph node. The same yellow Yo play mark identifies both the companion and the Videyo logo. The page uses generous space between creative beats and removes panels that repeat knowledge already visible in the graph.

The Yo play mark is one yellow right-facing triangle with the letters `yo` inside it. The `y` sits on the tall left side and the `o` follows before the triangle narrows. It is never a badge plus an arrow or a button with a separate play icon.

## Cohesion correction

Erin's direction: keep "yo, what happens next?" on one line, condense the opening, place the knowledge graph above the fold, move the sample story below it, and replace redundant project labeling with a clear statement that Videyo is the friend who helps bring videos to life and notices connections behind the scenes.
